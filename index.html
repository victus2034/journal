How this is worked out

How trades are built from a broker report

The report lists events, not trades: orders, and funding paid or received every few hours while a position is open. Rows are grouped by pair in time order. An order with no P&L (only a fee) opens a trade or adds to it; an order with P&L closes part or all of it. The next fee-only order after a close starts a new trade. Funding goes to the trade open on that pair at the time. Net P&L is the report's own net column (gross plus settlement, less fees). A trade with no closing order yet counts as open and stays out of the win rate. Times are as written in the report. Closed trades also count on the Dashboard, in goals and in the book's balance, on the day they closed; open ones wait for their close.

Trading Day

A new trading day starts at this time, not at midnight. 05:30 IST is 00:00 UTC, when exchanges reset their day. A trade before it counts for the day before on the dashboard, calendar, daily limits, goals and Analysis. Each trade keeps its real date and time. Saved on this device.

CoinDCX Futures Import

Not set up

CoinDCX doesn't answer web pages, so GitHub reads it for you: every hour a job in your sync repo signs in with your key and adds new futures transactions (INR and USDT margined) to the journal file. They arrive with the next sync, in the CoinDCX book an uploaded report fills; rows a report already has count once.

CoinDCX has no read-only keys, so this key can also place orders. Make a separate key just for the journal, keep it only in GitHub's secrets, and don't bind it to an IP address: GitHub's servers change address.
