# Victus Journal

An intraday trading journal and workstation in a single file, `index.html`. No build step and no server: open the file in a browser (desktop or phone) and it runs.

## Layout

Every tab starts with its title and one line of filters, then one row of section tabs (a line under the open one); only the picked section shows.

**Look (v.132)**: one font (Inter) for text and numbers, cards with a hairline border and no shadows or gradients, sentence-case labels, and number rows drawn as one card split by thin lines instead of a box per number. Number rows (v.173, lakky: "hard to see" on an iPad) are 2 across on a phone, 4 on a tablet or laptop and 8 only from 1440px; a value stays on one line and shrinks to fit its card if it must (`fitStatRow`, re-run when a row is redrawn or changes width), the line under it wraps instead of ending in "…", and two-figure cards (Best / worst, Best / worst day, Avg win / loss, Fees / funding) put one figure under the other with its coin, day or name (`statPair`). Picked filter chips are a soft amber tint; solid amber is kept for the main buttons. Colours come from a few tokens (`--bg`, `--surface`, `--line`, `--txt`, `--mut`, `--dim`, `--acc` in `#clean-theme`), and the light theme only swaps them. On a phone the header is one line (logo, theme, balance) with the books under it.

- **Dashboard**: the **goal** (progress bar, its targets, to go today) and **Today** side by side on top. Today shows the day's P&L, a **Daily loss used** bar (today's loss against the daily loss limit: green, amber from 60%, red at 100%), risk used, trades and the max loss. Then the **month** (its picker and the 8 numbers), then the sections: **Equity & calendar** · **Day's trades** · **Week & grade** (week summary and process grade) · **Time & habits** (one short row per half-hour slot you traded, read down each column in time order, and the confidence levels, mistakes and what went right as rows: count, win rate, a bar for the size of the result and the money; clicking a row opens Day's trades with just those trades).
- **Signals**: **Load scanner alerts** on top (Paste signals and Delete all signals under **More**), then the Zone / Fib / Trendline switch and two panels, one labelled row per control (since v.167): **Which alerts** (Period, **Market** (All / Crypto / Indian, v.191: Crypto = every alert not on NSE, Indian = NSE; the Channel list then shows only that market's channels, and Indian hides Assets and Coins), a **Channel** dropdown that lists only channels with alerts, **Assets** (Both / Crypto / xStocks, v.189: Both = every alert, Crypto = coins only, xStocks = tokenised US stocks only; works together with Coins, e.g. xStocks + Delta), **Coins** (All / Top 3 / Top 5 / Picks / **CoinDCX** / **Delta**, v.189: CoinDCX / Delta = only what that broker's futures list, by the same lists the Demo account's real orders use - Delta's read live when it can be - so Signals and the Demo account count the same alerts; the row says how many it keeps and which symbols it leaves out; since v.191 an option that would keep nothing, e.g. CoinDCX or Top 3 with xStocks, is greyed and a tap says why, picking Assets / Market / Channel that empties the Coins pick puts it back to All with a note, and an empty view says which filter left nothing, with a button back, instead of a row of dashes), **Levels on** (L1-L4 switches: an off level leaves out the alerts that entered there, v.183) and **Level** for Fib alerts, **Type** for Trendline alerts, BTC day; the dates on the right) and **How each trade is played** (**Exit at**, **SL**, **Move SL**, **Partial**, **Trail TP**, **Hold**, **Fees**, and **Reset** back to the scanner's own results). Tap a panel's title to fold it: folded, it says its settings in one line (kept on the device). Then the headline numbers (Alerts, Filled, Win rate, Total R, Average R, Profit factor, **Max drawdown** with the worst losing run, **Best / worst**; why some alerts have no result folds under one line), then **Alerts** · **Backtest report** · **Demo account** · **Edge & breakdowns** · **Alert vs my trade** · **Market days**. Since v.182 the candles behind Exit at and the candle SLs are read from Delta in fixed blocks (1,800 candles of one size) shared by every alert on a coin and kept for the session: about a fifth of the reads (a month of fib alerts with a Body SL: ~1,040 reads before, ~225 now, same results); the alerts in view are read first when a setting changes mid-read, and a busy Delta (429 / 5xx) is asked again twice. Since v.184 a history (rebuilt) fib or trendline alert, stamped at the start of the candle the scanner checked, fills only after the first candle that reaches the alert band (0.75%, 3% on 1W / 1M): an order placed on the alert could not have filled before it went out. **v.194:** alerts for coins the scanner no longer watches are **deleted** (memory, IndexedDB, and every scanner read and rebuild skips them), so the tab loads less; the watchlist is read from the scanner's `config.py` (built-in list if it can't be read) and NSE is never dropped. The only kept copies are a dropped coin's alerts that one of your own trades matches (same day, or inside the alert's look window), so the trade keeps its setup. The v.193 Hidden / Shown switch is gone. **Top 10 / Top 20 / Top 30** rank by 30-day CoinDCX volume (14 Nov) and count only coins on **both** CoinDCX and Delta (the row names any that are not). **v.193:** Coins got Top 10 / 20 / 30; **Edge & breakdowns** opens with **Best settings**: Run search tries every option of every setting one at a time, finds the best full combination (min 30 trades), reads it on the newest 40% of alerts it never saw, and runs a random-settings luck check; **Use** puts the combination on the tab. 
- **Analysis**: laid out like Signals (2026-11-08): book and alert type (All / Zone / Fib / Trendline / No alert) on top, then two folding panels. **Which trades**: Period 1 Day / 7 Days / 1 Month / All / Custom, **Market** (All / Crypto / Indian by the trade's book, v.191), Channel, **Assets** (Both / Crypto / xStocks), **Coins** (All / Top 3 / Top 5 by market cap or by the coins you trade most / Picks / CoinDCX / Delta; NSE, gold, silver and US stocks are not coins), Level L1-L4 for Fib or Type for Trendline, BTC day, and **Clear**. **How each trade is played**: Exit at (**My exit**, or 1R / 1.5R / 2R / 3R / 1% / 2% / 3% for the demo below), Move SL, Trail TP, Hold (Till TP / SL, 1 day, 1 week) and Fees with / without, with **Reset**. Folded, each panel says in one line what it is set to (kept per device). A row of 8 numbers sits under them on every section: Net P&L (W/L, fees), Win rate (average win / loss), Average R, Profit factor, Max drawdown (worst losing run), Best / worst, Trades checked and Oversized. A trade's alert type and level come from its setup tag (FIB / TRENDLINE / ZONE and the level ticked, auto or by hand), else the alert behind it; a setup of your own or None counts as No alert; the channel is that alert's market and timeframe. "Without" fees adds each trade's fees back. Every number below follows them. **Overview** opens with **What the numbers say** (the best and worst setup, the strongest other pattern by side / timeframe / time of day, the rule whose breaks cost most, the costliest mistake, average win vs loss, and fees when they take over 15% of gross wins; groups of 3+ trades) beside **Results by** Setup / Side / Timeframe / Time of day / Weekday (trades, win rate, average R, net; click a setup to filter the tab to it). Results by broker sits at the bottom of Overview. Every book now opens on **Report** (Delta and All books too, built from your trades with the filters applied): its line gives the trades, dates, fees, funding and average hold (the headline numbers are the row above), then the equity curve, By pair (click one, or use the Pair dropdown, to filter the whole tab) and By day. Then **Overview** (Results by broker with a **Total** line, the size numbers, rule scorecard, size vs balance, mistakes) · **Demo vs you** · **Review (N)** (trades from the last 30 days still to review, every book: ✓ Clean, pick a mistake, or Review) · **Position size** · **Trades** (trade by trade; the full list with prices and Edit folds under it) · **Market days** · **AI review**. A broker-report book (CoinDCX) adds **Report** first (its own numbers, equity, pairs and days) and its report trades on top of **Trades**: one row of sections, no tabs inside tabs. An empty book (an unused Dhan) is left out here as on the header bar. The "broke a rule" count in the numbers is the rule scorecard's, so the two always agree. Chart labels keep the year when the trades span more than one year.
- **Calculator**: account, then **Quick size** · **Nifty options** · **Full calculator** · **SL ladder** · **Goal planner**. Until you pick an account it sizes from a real book (the one picked on the header, else the one you traded last), never a made-up $5,000; an account you pick, Custom included, stays.
- **Settings** (was Sync & Settings): **Books & rules** (Trading Books, Setup Playbook, Trading Day) · **Connections** (GitHub sync, Delta import, CoinDCX, AI Review key, Broker Report Files) · **Backup** (backup file, CSV, restore, purge, storage used).

## Tabs

- **Analysis demo mode** (lakky, 2026-11-08: "check what mistakes i did if i go with the tp and sl not with amount i saw and exit"): pick a target in Analysis → How each trade is played → Exit at (anything but **My exit**). Every trade with a stop (yours, else its alert's) is replayed from its own entry time and price on Delta's public candles (1-minute for the first day, then 15-minute up to ~20 days, then 4-hour; read only while the rules picked leave the trade open, 8 trades at a time), same size, closing at that target or its stop, moved by Move SL / Trail TP and closed by Hold like Signals' Exit at. A candle touching the stop and the target counts as the stop, a stop fills 0.05% past its price, and the plan pays the trade's own fees (none with Fees: Without). The numbers row turns into the demo's (Replayed, Demo net P&L, **Plan vs you**, Win rate, Average R, Profit factor, Max drawdown, Best / worst, each with yours beside it) and under it the rules as amber chips (Exit, Move SL, Trail TP, Hold, Back to my exits) with one short line on what it covers (no stop, still open, NSE, reading prices, Retry; hover for what each means). **Demo vs you** (opens when you pick a target) shows three figures, **Plan made**, **You made** and **Your exits cost** (or made more), then the **costliest habit** on one line, and groups each trade: **Closed before the TP** (the plan reached its target, you took less), **Lost past the stop**, **Plan kept more** (BE / SL shift / time exit), **About the same** (within 0.1R), **Cut before the stop** and **Beat the plan**; click a group to list its trades, then trade by trade (entry, stop, your P&L, R and exit, the plan's P&L, R and how it closed, plan vs you). Settings are Analysis's own (`state.ui.anPlay`; until changed, Move SL and Trail TP start as Signals has them). Walks are kept on the device with Signals' (IndexedDB, keys `an|…`): a finished one for good, one cut short at your rules until other rules need more of it, and one still open for 15 minutes, so opening Analysis again reads nothing new. Crypto only: NSE trades have no candles here.
- **Dashboard**: P&L, goals, time-of-day, confidence and mistake/positive tags. The **Review** section, now on Analysis (with the count waiting, e.g. "Review (5)"), lists every trade from the last 30 days not reviewed yet (no tag, rule, notes, confidence or Clean mark), journal and CoinDCX alike: tap **✓ Clean** if there's nothing to note, pick a mistake from the **Mistake…** dropdown (FOMO Entry, Early Exit, Moved Stop Loss, Revenge Trade) to tag it, or **Review** to open the inspector. Each tap takes the trade off the list. The week summary (P&L, win rate, avg R with the % move of those same trades, best setup, costliest mistake, P&L by day) is its own **Week & grade** section.
- **Process grade (weekly, no AI)**: under the week summary, an A–F grade for *how* you traded that week, not what you made, worked out from the trades with nothing to fill in. It lists stops used (yours or the alert's; trades with no stop known are counted, not hidden), size and loss inside your limits, reviewed trades with a mistake tagged, rules ticked, and revenge-sized trades with their cost. Three **behaviour scores** (0–100%) sit beside it, each with the last 8 weeks as small bars and the change from last week: **Discipline** (no revenge, size-up or trading past the daily limits; reviews without mistakes; rules ticked), **Risk management** (a stop known; size and loss inside your max risk) and **Consistency** (position size and risk % within half to 1.5× your usual for that book). A check with no data that week is left out instead of scoring 0. Grade: A 85%+, B 70%+, C 55%+, D 40%+, else F.
- **Revenge sizing**: a trade opened after 2 or more losses in a row that day (losses that had closed before it opened), with a position over 1.25× the average size of those losses. It is flagged in Analysis (Size & mistakes, rule scorecard's "calm" rule), breaks the "No revenge trades" goal streak, and is listed under the process grade with its P&L and the part of its loss that came from the extra size, plus a running 30-day total. Journal and CoinDCX trades alike.
- **Setup and level from the scanner's alerts**: every trade (journal and CoinDCX) is held against the scanner's fib and trendline alerts on its symbol, from when each was sent for a few candles of its timeframe (30m 1 day, 4h 4 days, 1D 10, 1W 30, 1M 60). It gets the setup (FIB or TRENDLINE, or the playbook setup of that name) and the level it was taken at: for a fib, the box edge its entry was nearest (L1-L4); for a trendline, SUPPORT (a long at the lower line), RESISTANCE (a short at the upper line), SP BROKEN (a short below the lower line, up to 3R under it) or RT BROKEN (a long above the upper line). The line's price at the entry minute comes from its two anchors, which the journal now keeps from the scanner's file. **When several alerts fit, the one whose entry price is closest to the trade's entry wins** (prices within 0.1% of the entry count as the same; then an alert Discord actually sent beats one only in the rebuilt history, then the freshest). Its level is where the entry sits on that alert's fib: a fib alert keyed at L1 / L2 / L4 (the scanner sends every level since 20 Oct) is measured from that level, not from L1 / L3. A trendline break alert only fits a trade on its own side, and a touch of a line no longer counts once that line broke before the trade. The broker's name can be longer than the scanner's (Delta MRVLBUSD, scanner MRVL). Fib and trendline alerts Discord sent before the rebuild's cutoff are hidden from Signals (the rebuilt history replaces them) but kept aside on the device, only to match your trades: the rebuilt history doesn't have every alert that went out (BTC's 1M L2 of 30 Oct is not in it). When nothing fits, the alert the trade was matched to (same symbol, side and day) still gives its setup, and a zone alert gives ZONE; when several alert types paired with it that day, the one with the closest entry. A journal trade with no stop also gets its matched alert's stop (so its R and risk work), and Analysis uses that same alert for a trade's channel, its alert stop and "No alert behind it". Only what was filled in automatically is changed later: a setup you picked, a level you ticked, or None stays. It runs when trades, alerts or the playbook change, and before a sync sends. The inspector shows "stop from alert" and "(from alert)". The scanner's fib and trendline alerts start on 29 Sep 2026, so older trades can't be placed.
- **Signals**: imported alerts by day, setup, score, outcome, symbol, channel, sector, timeframe and hour, with R and % move. Beside the R totals (Total R, Average R, Alerts made, left on the table) the % reads *price move* (v.177): each alert's move from entry (R × its stop size) added up, with no position size or leverage, so it can be negative while R is positive (losers with wider stops than winners) and is not an account return; the Demo account gives that. Alerts stay on each device. The journal reads the scanner's files by itself when it opens, when Signals opens, when its tab comes back into view and every 10 minutes while it stays open (a failed read is tried again 2 minutes later, and the line under the title says so; new alerts wait to redraw while you're typing; from a week before the newest alert the device has, or further back to the oldest fib, trendline or 1D+ zone alert still open, up to 90 days, so a 1W or 1M result that closes weeks later still arrives) and adds new alerts and results, so recent trades always have alerts to match. Fib, trendline and 1D/1W/1M S/R zone results are priced with the scanner's costs (Delta's 0.0826% fee for crypto, 0.1063% for NSE, the breakeven offset and the 0.05% stop slippage). A fib or trendline is out in full at the TP its alert prints, 1R (v.180, lakky: "on TP it should consider that fully"): one that reached 1R is +1R whatever came after, and one that reached 2R is +1R too; BE only where the scanner's breakeven stop closed it before the TP. A 1D+ zone is held for 2R with the breakeven stop, so one that reached 1R and came back is the breakeven or stop that closed it, and a timeout after 1R has no R. (v.179 held fibs for 2R the same way; before v.179 a fib's 2R was +2R.), and an alert already loaded is re-priced when that changes. 1D+ zones show under Zone alerts. **30m S/R zone alerts are not loaded or kept** (the scanner turned them off on 16 Oct 2026). Zone alerts from 1 Sep 2026 up to the rebuild's cutoff come from the scanner's **rebuilt zone backtest** (`zone_rebuild_*` files: every 4H / 1D / 1W / 1M zone alert today's rules would have sent, graded by today's scoring, fills within 15 min of US CPI / PPI / FOMC left out); zone alerts graded under older rules are dropped from the device when a rebuild arrives, and live zone alerts after the cutoff load as before. Fib and trendline alerts work the same way with the scanner's **rebuilt fib/trendline backtest** (`fibtl_rebuild_*`: every fib level and trendline touch today's rules would have sent since **1 Jun 2026**, Sep 1 until 7 Nov 2026). The rebuilt files are several MB, so a device downloads them once per rebuild (and once per app version); later refreshes read only their small meta files and skip them while the rebuild is unchanged, as long as the device still holds it: it counts the rebuilt alerts it holds against how many it took (per market), and one that lost part of them (Delete all signals, a cleared store) takes the whole rebuild again (v.178; before, such a device kept only the last month, and fewer each day). **Load scanner alerts** is still there to load a longer range by hand; its *How far back* applies to live alerts, and the rebuilt history comes with it whenever the device lacks it. The line under the Signals title shows the newest alert and when it last checked. Under **Fib alerts**, **Level** (All · L1 · L2 · L3 · L4, worked out from each alert's fib) backtests one level: every fib alert as if it entered there, with its own stop. An alert that entered at that level keeps its result; one that entered at another level takes the scanner's daily what-if for it (`fib_level_results.json`, marked *what-if Lx* in the table; not reached = no fill); alerts with neither are left out. The scanner enters fibs at L1 or L3, so L2 and L4 are all what-ifs. Every number on the tab follows it (headline, calendar, Backtest report, Edge & breakdowns, Exit at, Fees); it is hidden for Zone and Trendline alerts. Under **Trendline alerts**, **Type** (All · Support TL · Resistance TL · SP TL BR · RS TL BR) keeps one kind of trendline alert: longs at the lower line, shorts at the upper line, shorts through the lower line or longs through the upper line (line from the alert key, plus side). Everything on the tab follows it. The scanner does not send break alerts yet, so the two break types stay empty until it does. Your trades are held against the alerts: journal trades plus CoinDCX trades, whose side comes from the fills, or from the fees when the fills haven't come in yet (a trade from today). **My trades with no alert** lists every one in the period (paged only past 50). A **Backtest report** section plays every alert with a result in order, as if each was taken at 1R: win rate, expectancy, profit factor, average win and loss, payoff ratio, max drawdown and how long it lasted, recovery factor, win and loss streaks, spread (SD) and SQN, fill rate and alerts a day; an equity curve with its drawdown (v.176: R axis in round steps, the start dashed, dates under it, deepest fall marked; hover, tap or drag for each alert: when, coin, side, R, how it ended, total and how far under the high); the spread of results and how they ended (+2R, +1R, BE, SL); **What to cut** (groups of 5+ alerts that lost R, and what the rest made without them); **If you skip thin stops** (the same numbers keeping only stops at least 0.2% to 1% wide); and a breakdown by channel, symbol, side, stop width, score, hour, weekday, week, month or BTC day, sortable by any column. **How sure is this?** under the headline figures: the sample (too few under 30, early read under 100, fair, solid from 300), the 95% range of the average R (mean ± t × SD ÷ √n, t ≈ 2 and wider on a small sample: above zero, below zero or could still be zero) and of the win rate (Wilson, against the break-even win rate at this payoff), the first half vs the second half of the alerts in time order, months and weeks in profit, **time in trade** (median and average, fill to close, from the scanner's own fill and close times; with Exit at Scanner only) and trades **still open** (not counted; with a fixed Exit at, where they stand at the last price read). **Top coins vs all crypto** (lakky, 2026-11-07) splits the same alerts, before the Coins filter, into All crypto, Top 3, Top 5, Other coins (not top 5) and Your picks, then **Markets and brokers** (xStocks, gold / silver / oil, On CoinDCX, On Delta; v.189), then each top coin: alerts, graded, win %, average R with its 95% range, total R, profit factor, max drawdown and the longest losing run; under it, Top 3 / Top 5 / picks vs the rest with the gap in average R and whether it is *likely real* (Welch t ≥ 2, each side 10+ results), *maybe* (t ≥ 1) or *could be luck*. **Export CSV** saves the alerts in the view with their results. It follows the period, channel, coins and BTC-day filters. **Coins** (All · Top 3 · Top 5 · Picks) keeps only those coins' alerts everywhere on the tab: Top 3 / Top 5 by market cap (BTC, ETH, XRP, BNB, SOL; stablecoins left out) or by the most alerts in view, Picks = coins you tick (BTC, ETH, SOL to start, each with its alert count). Gold, silver, US stocks and NSE are not coins, so they are left out while it is on; with no crypto in view it is off. **Exit at** (Scanner · 1R · 1.5R · 2R · 3R · 1% · 2% · 3%) re-scores every graded alert as if it closed at that one fixed target, with its own stop and no breakeven move: the headline numbers, calendar, Edge & breakdowns (By outcome then shows Target / SL / Time exit) and Alert vs my trade all follow it. Crypto prices come from Delta's public candles, **always 15m** (v.186, lakky 2026-11-12: "most of the times its differ"): until then the candle size came from the alert's age (1m / 5m / 15m, then 1h-1d), so one alert scored differently depending on the day it was first walked, and walks kept by older builds are walked again once. Candles are read in blocks of 1,800 shared by every alert on a coin, only as far as each walk needs, at most 6 reads at a time, and blocks that have ended are kept on the device (IndexedDB `tgtc|` keys, the 600 used last), so the next visit reads only the newest block. While prices load, or when some alerts have none, a **Not final** line above the numbers says how many are left out. Export CSV gives the numbers the tab shows (with Exit at, the what-if R; it used to give the scanner's) plus the rules, Delta symbol, fill time, exit and minutes held, for checking a retest trade by trade. `tools/signals-retest.py` is an independent retest written from the rules (candle by candle on 15m; NSE on 60m). **NSE alerts** (v.190, lakky 2026-11-13: NSE 1D trendlines showed only dashes) are played on the same rules as crypto, on Yahoo's candles: Delta has no NSE stocks and Yahoo doesn't answer web pages, so `tools/nse-candles.mjs`, run twice a day by the sync repo's `nse-paths.yml` Action (*NSE candles*), reads every NSE symbol the scanner alerts on from Yahoo and publishes its candles as the sync repo's `nse-candles` branch (one commit, no history): per symbol `SYM.a.json` (before the previous month, changes once a month) and `SYM.b.json` (the rest), each with Yahoo's 60m candles (the walks, from 25 May 2026) and NSE's own 4H (09:15-13:15 and 13:15-15:30 IST), 1D, 1W (Monday weeks) and 1M candles (the candle SL), float32 prices written short. The journal reads the branch's file list with the sync token (at most every 30 min) and each file by blob sha, kept on the device (IndexedDB `nsec|` keys) until it changes, so NSE needs GitHub sync on (the line under the numbers says so, or that the job hasn't run yet). For NSE "20 candles" (the fill window and the 20-candle hold) counts NSE candles of the alert's timeframe, so nights, weekends and holidays don't count. Every SL type, Move SL, Trail TP, Partial, Hold and the Demo account work on NSE alerts; NSE alerts under 4H have no Exit at result. Until v.190 the job wrote one summary per graded alert (`nse-paths.json`, no longer read or written): only the alert's own stop could be scored, so any other SL left every NSE alert out. NSE fees are 0.1063%. Each alert is read from its alert time until 20 candles of its own timeframe after the fill; a candle touching both the stop and the target counts as the stop, and a target not hit by then closes at that candle's close (Time exit). Costs as the scanner: 0.0826% round trip and 0.05% stop slippage. Finished alerts' candles are read once and kept on the device. **Move SL** (Off · BE · SL shift) moves the stop on top of any fixed Exit at: **BE** puts it at entry once price reaches the trigger (+0.5R to +3R, 1R by default); **SL shift** puts it at a level you pick once price reaches the trigger (e.g. at +1R lock +0.75R; levels from -0.5R up to just under the trigger). With an R target, triggers at or above it are greyed out (the target comes first, so the stop would never move) and a saved one past it is flagged in amber; With a % target (1% / 2% / 3%) the trigger and the level are % moves from entry too (at +0.25% to +3%, to -0.25% up to just under the trigger; 1% → +0.5% by default), and the same greying applies. **SL** (lakky, 2026-11-10: "so many ways to see sl ... below or above candle, wick"; v.181) picks the stop each alert is played with, on top of any fixed Exit at (picking one with Exit at on Scanner moves it to 1R; back on Scanner the alert's own SL and result stand, and a line says so): **Alert** (its own SL, the default), **Wick** (the most recent closed candle of the alert's own timeframe, before the alert, whose wick, the low for a buy or the high for a sell, is at least 0.25% / 0.5% / 0.75% / 1% / 1.5% past the entry; the stop sits on that wick), **Body** (the same with the candle body, open or close), **Wick or body** (the closer of the two: the scanner's trendline stop at 0.75%, the same stop on 2,000 random charts), **Fixed %** (0.5% to 3% from entry) and, on Fib alerts, **Next level** (just past the next deeper level, 25% of a box: the scanner's 4H / 1D rule since 27 Oct, the same stop on all 819 live alerts since) and **L4 box** (every level on the strong box's stop, the rule before). Candles come from Delta (NSE: the NSE job's own 4H / 1D / 1W / 1M candles, closed before the alert): up to 300 candles of the alert's timeframe before it (1W built from days from Monday, 1M from days by calendar month), read once per alert and kept with the walks (IndexedDB, keys `slc|…`, a few numbers each: every new low or high of the wicks and bodies going back, which serve every level and gap). Each alert is then walked again with its new stop, so R is measured on that stop and the headline, calendar, breakdowns, Move SL, Trail TP, Hold, Fees and the Demo account follow it; the alerts table tags each row with the SL in play (its Signal R stays the scanner's, on the alert's own SL). Alerts with no candle that far back have no result under Wick / Body; the line under the numbers says how many (NSE alerts had none at all before v.190). Edge & breakdowns adds **SL compared** under Move SL compared: once you press *Read prices for every SL* (each SL is its own walk), total R for every SL type × every fixed target on the same alerts, with each type's average SL size and Move SL / Trail TP as picked; best underlined; tap a cell to use that SL and target. **Trail TP** (Off · On) lets a fixed target run: each time price reaches the target, the target moves up a step (+0.25R / +0.5R / +1R) and the stop goes to a gap behind the target just reached (at the TP, 0.25R, 0.5R, 0.75R or 1R behind) but never below the first target (once hit, the TP is locked), from the next candle; e.g. target 1.5R, +0.25R, SL 0.5R behind: at 1.5R the stop goes to +1.5R, at 1.75R and 2R it stays there, at 2.25R it goes to +1.75R, and so on until the stop is hit (By outcome: Trail TP) or Hold ends. With a % target the steps and gaps are % moves. Move SL works up to the target (its triggers at or past the target stay greyed out and never fire); after the target Trail TP moves the stop, and where both apply the higher stop counts. The compare tables apply Trail TP to every cell. **Partial** (v.188; Off · 25% · 50% · 75%) books that share of the position at a level you pick (+0.5R to +3R, or +0.25% to +3% with a % target; a limit order, no slippage) on the first candle that reaches it, after the stop check; the rest runs to the TP or the stop (moved by Move SL / Trail TP). With Move SL on, **book** (nothing · 25% · 50% · 75%) closes that share at the BE / SL shift trigger and the rest goes on with the moved stop. Shares are of the whole position, never more than all of it (75% at +0.5R and 50% at BE closes the last 25% at BE: *Partial*). Without Trail TP a level at or past the target books nothing (the target closes it all, greyed out); with Trail TP it books on the way up. Fees stay one round trip on the whole position. Exits read *Part + Target*, *Part + BE*, *Part + SL* and so on in By outcome and the CSV; the headline, compare tables, Demo account and CSV all follow it; `tools/signals-retest.py` plays it too (the parity test holds journal = retest). The "till TP / SL" walk goes on past the biggest target (3R / 3%) until price falls back 2R / 2% from its best on a later candle, so every Trail TP stop is settled (summaries kept before it, without `tr`, are read once more). **Hold** (Till TP / SL · 20 candles) sets how long a fixed-target trade stays open: **Till TP / SL** (the default) reads from the alert to now and keeps each trade until its target or its (moved) stop, however long it takes; one with neither yet has no result and the headline says how many are still open. Candles: a window that fits 1,900 15m candles (or finer) is one read; a longer one (an alert older than ~19 days on Till TP / SL, 1D+ alerts on 20 candles) reads 15m candles for its first ~18 days and the finest size that fits the rest after (v.179; before, one read in 1h-1d candles hid the pullbacks inside each candle and Trail TP read far better than it was). Walks kept from before are read once more. **20 candles** is the scanner's window: closed at the 20th candle of the alert's timeframe after the fill (Time exit, under By outcome). Either way the fill must come within 20 candles of the alert. With a fixed Exit at, that walk decides whether an alert (crypto, or NSE since v.190) filled, not the scanner: an alert the scanner called *no fill* (its fib wait is 5 candles) that reached the entry within the 20 candles is scored too, and Filled / no fill / waiting follow the walk; one not reached while its 20 candles are still running is read again later instead of being kept as not filled (lakky, 2026-11-08: BTC L4 1D of 15 Oct filled on 30 Oct and was stopped, and was missing). Each hold keeps its own walk (`tgtHoldKey`, the "till TP / SL" one under `<key>|tp`). The stop moves from the candle after the one that reached the trigger (the scanner's breakeven rule); a candle touching the moved stop and the target counts as the stop, and the moved stop slips 0.05% like any stop. Everything Exit at drives follows it (headline, calendar, breakdowns, By outcome shows BE / SL shift, Backtest report, Demo account). Scanner keeps the scanner's own rules. For this each alert's walk keeps a small price path (each new best move in R with the worst move before it, `tgtRec`); summaries kept before it are read once more. ## Input checks

Typed values are checked before they are saved, and the same rules run on anything loaded from a backup or GitHub:

- A trade needs entry, exit and quantity above 0, and price × quantity under 10^15 (bigger is a typo that would blank every total).
- Trade and withdrawal dates run from 2000 to today. Symbols keep 30 letters.
- A goal's ticked target must be a number above 0. A book's max risk is 100% at most, and max trades a day at least 1.

## Where data lives

- **This browser**: the journal is saved in `localStorage` (`tapeAndTarget.v2`; `tapeAndTarget.mktHourly` for cached BTC candles). Alerts are kept in IndexedDB (database `tapeAndTarget`, key `tapeAndTarget.signals`), which holds far more than localStorage's ~5 MB, so a long alert history can no longer fill the space trades need. Alerts an older build left in localStorage move to IndexedDB on the next open; a browser without IndexedDB keeps them in localStorage as before. The candle summaries behind Signals' Exit at / Move SL / Trail TP live in the same IndexedDB store (key `tapeAndTarget.sigTgt`; an older build's localStorage copy moves across, and without IndexedDB they stay in localStorage only while under 1 MB). Offline, no prices are read (Signals says so and reads them when the connection is back); with Delta unreachable the reads stop after a dozen failures and Retry picks them up. The storage line in Settings → Backup counts localStorage only and shows the alerts' size beside it. Clearing site data clears the journal.
- **Broker report uploads**: stored with the journal (`backtest` in `tapeAndTarget.v2` and in `journal.json` once something is uploaded): the report's rows only, never the cover sheet's name, email or PAN. Each uploaded file keeps the broker it belongs to; the broker's book (`rep-<broker>-<currency>`) is made from it on any device. The file is read in the browser; nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Deleted records**: a deletion leaves a marker so sync doesn't bring the record back from another device. Markers are dropped after 30 days (183 for a deleted Delta trade's order tag, which stops the Delta import re-adding it), so `journal.json` stays small. A device not synced for over 30 days could bring back something deleted since.
- **GitHub sync (optional)**: in Settings → Connections, add your sync repo (`owner/repo`) and a fine-grained token with *Contents: Read and write* on it. The journal is saved there as `journal.json` on the `main` branch (there is no Branch field; a setup saved on another branch by an older build keeps it), so other devices can pick it up. Both fields show as dots, with plain hints, so the screen never shows the repo name or token. The token and webhook never leave the browser; the Gemini key only inside `keysLock` once you turn that on (next point); the Delta and CoinDCX keys live only in the sync repo's Actions secrets, for the PC's imports.
- **Keys on your other devices** (in the GitHub sync card): each browser keeps its own storage (on an iPhone the home-screen app doesn't share Safari's), so a new device used to have no Gemini key. **Save keys to GitHub** asks for a passphrase (8+ letters, typed twice) and saves the Gemini key in `journal.json` as `keysLock`: AES-256-GCM under a key made from the passphrase with PBKDF2-SHA256 (600,000 rounds, random salt), so GitHub only holds the sealed copy; the passphrase is never saved or sent. A device that connects GitHub and finds the lock asks for the passphrase once (or unlock it in the card) and fills the keys in. It keeps the derived key in its own sync settings (never synced), so a key saved later on any device fills in on the others at their next sync: a key that changed in the lock is filled in where that device's copy is empty or still the old one; a key you changed or removed on one device stays as you left it there. **Set up another device** (there in the Off state too: it asks for the passphrase first) shows a setup code, a link (`#vjsetup=…`, taken out of the address bar at once) and its QR code: the repo and token sealed the same way. Paste the code in **Use setup code** (or in Repository Name) with the passphrase and GitHub connects and the keys fill in; on an iPhone, copy it in Safari and paste it in the home-screen app. A code for a different repo than the device uses asks first. **Forgot it? Start over** replaces the lock with this device's keys under a new passphrase (other devices keep theirs and ask for the new one). **Take keys off GitHub** leaves an `off` marker; every device keeps the keys it has. Disconnect forgets the passphrase key on that device. Backup files never include `keysLock`. Delta and CoinDCX need nothing here: their keys are in the sync repo's Actions secrets. A lock an older build made with the Delta key inside is resealed without it by the first device that opens it.
- **Delta balance across devices**: the PC's Delta import writes Delta's wallet figure to `journal.json` as `deltaBalance` (when it moves, or hourly), and every device shows it instead of its own trade sum. The header tag shows the date and time the figure was read, in India time (e.g. **14 Nov, 8:30 PM**): green while it is under 2 hours old, amber after that (import not running), and Trading Books says "Delta wallet from …" then. With several books in view (All accounts) the time is that of the oldest stale figure, or the newest read when none is stale. Its tooltip says more. No browser fetches it with a key any more.
- **Delta through the PC** (the only Delta import since v2026-11-03.151; the Delta card has no key boxes, Test & Connect or in-page Import Now / Full History): Delta only answers a key from the IPs whitelisted on it, and a phone's IP (Jio, IPv4 and IPv6) keeps changing, so an import in the browser failed there with `ip_not_whitelisted_for_api_key`. The sync repo's `delta.yml` Action runs `tools/delta-import.mjs` on lakky's PC (the self-hosted runner that runs the CoinDCX import, label `coindcx`, IPv4 only) with a Read Data key whitelisted for the PC's public IP, kept in the sync repo's secrets (`DELTA_API_KEY`, `DELTA_API_SECRET`). It rebuilds trades from the fills (as the old in-page import did), adds the new ones to `journal.json`, keeps where it left off as `deltaImport` (newest fill read, positions still open, the reason a run failed) and writes the wallet figure to `deltaBalance`. Each trade carries its Delta order tag, so a trade either import brought in, or one deleted (its tag's tombstone), is never added twice; the first run reads the whole history once. A refused IP names the IP Delta saw (or the PC's, from api.ipify.org). The Delta card shows its state (Not set up / Importing / Last run failed) and the steps; a run GitHub calls a success that wrote nothing (no secrets yet) says Delta isn't set up, not "nothing new". A Delta key an older build saved on the device shows **Copy key** / **Copy secret** for the two secrets, and is deleted from the device by itself once the PC import has worked (or with **Delete it**). **Undo Delta Trades** removes them and makes the PC's next run read the whole history again. **Import now** (CoinDCX card, Delta card, Signals → More → Import CoinDCX + Delta) starts both jobs from any device, watches each and syncs as each finishes; Delta's job missing from the sync repo just means it isn't set up. While the journal is open it also starts Delta's job when the last run is an hour old, as for CoinDCX.
- **Gemini key**: kept in this browser (`tapeAndTarget.gemini`), outside the journal, so it is never put in backups and reaches `journal.json` only inside `keysLock`, when Keys on your other devices is on; the last AI review is kept in `tapeAndTarget.aiReview`. A review sends that period's trades (never any key or token) to Google.
- **Export**: Settings → Backup can download trades as CSV.

## Updating

Replace `index.html` and bump `<meta name="app-version">` near the top, so the version pill in the header shows which build each device is on. Add an entry for the new version at the top of `CHANGELOG` (same version, a short title, 1-3 bullets); the smoke test fails if the newest entry doesn't match the meta version.
Chart.js loads from jsDelivr; without it, charts are skipped and the rest still works. Its charts (Dashboard equity, broker report equity, position size) answer a hover or tap anywhere over them, not only on a dot (v.176): the Dashboard's tip gives the full date, the total (or drawdown), that day's P&L and trades with wins / losses, and how far under the high; the report's gives the trade's P&L, fees, time held, total and distance from the high; the size chart adds the trade's P&L. Zero is drawn stronger. The Signals and Demo account curves are drawn by the page itself; a tap's tip stays until you tap elsewhere (iPad).

## Checks

Every push runs `tests/smoke.mjs` on GitHub (the downloaded Chromium is cached between runs, and the job allows 25 min because GitHub's browser download is sometimes slow; the tests themselves stop at 10 min): it opens the page in a headless browser, with an empty journal and with made-up sample trades, on desktop and phone widths, opens every tab, and fails on any script error. It also uploads a made-up trade report (`tests/backtest-fixture.mjs`, as .xlsx and as .csv) and checks every trade and total against the report, in Analysis and on the Dashboard, plus the broker books and the per-broker results. The charts test serves Chart.js from `node_modules` (a dev dependency at the page's exact version and integrity hash) in place of the CDN. To run it locally: `npm install`, `npx playwright install chromium`, then `npm test` (or `npm run test:twice`, which runs it twice; CI and the VS Code "Test twice" task use that). AI editors follow `AGENTS.md`: every change is tested twice before it is called done. **Signals parity**: real scanner alerts (195, 10-25 Oct 2026) and Delta's real candles (`tests/fixtures/signals-parity.json.gz`) go through the Signals tab under 7 Exit at / SL / Move SL / Trail TP / Hold / Fees settings, and every trade must equal `tools/signals-retest.py` (needs `python3`), so the journal and a retest can no longer drift apart.

The clean-look check opens the sample journal and confirms the Daily loss used bar (a $1.00 loss on a $2.50 limit is 40% and green, $2.00 is 80% and amber), the dashboard tabs and their review count, the channel dropdown (only channels with alerts, and picking one filters), the Mistake… dropdown tagging a trade, and that no monospace font is left on the dashboard.
