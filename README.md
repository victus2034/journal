# Victus Journal

An intraday trading journal and workstation in a single file, `index.html`. No build step and no server: open the file in a browser (desktop or phone) and it runs.

## Layout

Every tab starts with its title and one line of filters, then one row of section tabs (a line under the open one); only the picked section shows.

**Look (v.132)**: one font (Inter) for text and numbers, cards with a hairline border and no shadows or gradients, sentence-case labels, and number rows drawn as one card split by thin lines instead of a box per number. Picked filter chips are a soft amber tint; solid amber is kept for the main buttons. Colours come from a few tokens (`--bg`, `--surface`, `--line`, `--txt`, `--mut`, `--dim`, `--acc` in `#clean-theme`), and the light theme only swaps them. On a phone the header is one line (logo, theme, balance) with the books under it.

- **Dashboard**: the **goal** (progress bar, its targets, to go today) and **Today** side by side on top. Today shows the day's P&L, a **Daily loss used** bar (today's loss against the daily loss limit: green, amber from 60%, red at 100%), risk used, trades and the max loss. Then the **month** (its picker and the 8 numbers), then the sections: **Equity & calendar** · **Day's trades** · **Week & grade** (week summary and process grade) · **Time & habits** (one short row per half-hour slot you traded, read down each column in time order, and the confidence levels, mistakes and what went right as rows: count, win rate, a bar for the size of the result and the money; clicking a row opens Day's trades with just those trades).
- **Signals**: **Load scanner alerts** on top (Paste signals and Delete all signals under **More**), then the Zone / Fib / Trendline switch and two panels, one labelled row per control (since v.167): **Which alerts** (Period, a **Channel** dropdown that lists only channels with alerts, **Coins**, **Level** for Fib alerts, **Type** for Trendline alerts, BTC day; the dates on the right) and **How each trade is played** (**Exit at**, **Move SL**, **Trail TP**, **Hold**, **Fees**, and **Reset** back to the scanner's own results). Tap a panel's title to fold it: folded, it says its settings in one line (kept on the device). Then the headline numbers (Alerts, Filled, Win rate, Total R, Average R, Profit factor, **Max drawdown** with the worst losing run, **Best / worst**; why some alerts have no result folds under one line), then **Alerts** · **Backtest report** · **Demo account** · **Edge & breakdowns** · **Alert vs my trade** · **Market days**.
- **Analysis**: filters (book; the Signals tab's set on your own trades: alert type All / Zone / Fib / Trendline / No alert, period 1 Day / 7 Days / 1 Month / All / Custom, channel, BTC day, Fees with / without, and Level L1-L4 for Fib or Type for Trendline). A trade's alert type and level come from its setup tag (FIB / TRENDLINE / ZONE and the level ticked, auto or by hand), else the alert behind it; a setup of your own or None counts as No alert; the channel is that alert's market and timeframe. "Without" fees adds each trade's fees back. The filters sit in two rows (books, alert type, period; then Channel, Level / Type, BTC day and Fees as small dropdowns, with **Clear filters**). Every number below follows them. **Overview** opens with Net P&L, Win rate, Average R, Profit factor, Trades checked and Oversized, then **What the numbers say** (the best and worst setup, the strongest other pattern by side / timeframe / time of day, the rule whose breaks cost most, the costliest mistake, average win vs loss, and fees when they take over 15% of gross wins; groups of 3+ trades) beside **Results by** Setup / Side / Timeframe / Time of day / Weekday (trades, win rate, average R, net; click a setup to filter the tab to it). Results by broker sits at the bottom of Overview. Every book now opens on **Report** (Delta and All books too, built from your trades with the filters applied): Net P&L, Win rate, Profit factor, Avg win / loss, Best / worst with streaks, Max drawdown, Fees / funding with average hold, and Average R, then the equity curve, By pair (click one, or use the Pair dropdown, to filter the whole tab) and By day. Then **Overview** (Results by broker with a **Total** line, the size numbers, rule scorecard, size vs balance, mistakes) · **Review (N)** (trades from the last 30 days still to review, every book: ✓ Clean, pick a mistake, or Review) · **Position size** · **Trades** (trade by trade; the full list with prices and Edit folds under it) · **Market days** · **AI review**. A broker-report book (CoinDCX) adds **Report** first (its own numbers, equity, pairs and days) and its report trades on top of **Trades**: one row of sections, no tabs inside tabs. An empty book (an unused Dhan) is left out here as on the header bar. The "broke a rule" count in the numbers is the rule scorecard's, so the two always agree. Chart labels keep the year when the trades span more than one year.
- **Calculator**: account, then **Quick size** · **Nifty options** · **Full calculator** · **SL ladder** · **Goal planner**. Until you pick an account it sizes from a real book (the one picked on the header, else the one you traded last), never a made-up $5,000; an account you pick, Custom included, stays.
- **Settings** (was Sync & Settings): **Books & rules** (Trading Books, Setup Playbook, Trading Day) · **Connections** (GitHub sync, Delta import, CoinDCX, AI Review key, Broker Report Files) · **Backup** (backup file, CSV, restore, purge, storage used).

## Tabs

- **Dashboard**: P&L, goals, time-of-day, confidence and mistake/positive tags. The **Review** section, now on Analysis (with the count waiting, e.g. "Review (5)"), lists every trade from the last 30 days not reviewed yet (no tag, rule, notes, confidence or Clean mark), journal and CoinDCX alike: tap **✓ Clean** if there's nothing to note, pick a mistake from the **Mistake…** dropdown (FOMO Entry, Early Exit, Moved Stop Loss, Revenge Trade) to tag it, or **Review** to open the inspector. Each tap takes the trade off the list. The week summary (P&L, win rate, avg R with the % move of those same trades, best setup, costliest mistake, P&L by day) is its own **Week & grade** section.
- **Process grade (weekly, no AI)**: under the week summary, an A–F grade for *how* you traded that week, not what you made, worked out from the trades with nothing to fill in. It lists stops used (yours or the alert's; trades with no stop known are counted, not hidden), size and loss inside your limits, reviewed trades with a mistake tagged, rules ticked, and revenge-sized trades with their cost. Three **behaviour scores** (0–100%) sit beside it, each with the last 8 weeks as small bars and the change from last week: **Discipline** (no revenge, size-up or trading past the daily limits; reviews without mistakes; rules ticked), **Risk management** (a stop known; size and loss inside your max risk) and **Consistency** (position size and risk % within half to 1.5× your usual for that book). A check with no data that week is left out instead of scoring 0. Grade: A 85%+, B 70%+, C 55%+, D 40%+, else F.
- **Revenge sizing**: a trade opened after 2 or more losses in a row that day (losses that had closed before it opened), with a position over 1.25× the average size of those losses. It is flagged in Analysis (Size & mistakes, rule scorecard's "calm" rule), breaks the "No revenge trades" goal streak, and is listed under the process grade with its P&L and the part of its loss that came from the extra size, plus a running 30-day total. Journal and CoinDCX trades alike.
- **Setup and level from the scanner's alerts**: every trade (journal and CoinDCX) is held against the scanner's fib and trendline alerts on its symbol, from when each was sent for a few candles of its timeframe (30m 1 day, 4h 4 days, 1D 10, 1W 30, 1M 60). It gets the setup (FIB or TRENDLINE, or the playbook setup of that name) and the level it was taken at: for a fib, the box edge its entry was nearest (L1-L4); for a trendline, SUPPORT (a long at the lower line), RESISTANCE (a short at the upper line), SP BROKEN (a short below the lower line, up to 3R under it) or RT BROKEN (a long above the upper line). The line's price at the entry minute comes from its two anchors, which the journal now keeps from the scanner's file. **When several alerts fit, the one whose entry price is closest to the trade's entry wins** (prices within 0.1% of the entry count as the same; then an alert Discord actually sent beats one only in the rebuilt history, then the freshest). Its level is where the entry sits on that alert's fib: a fib alert keyed at L1 / L2 / L4 (the scanner sends every level since 20 Oct) is measured from that level, not from L1 / L3. A trendline break alert only fits a trade on its own side, and a touch of a line no longer counts once that line broke before the trade. The broker's name can be longer than the scanner's (Delta MRVLBUSD, scanner MRVL). Fib and trendline alerts Discord sent before the rebuild's cutoff are hidden from Signals (the rebuilt history replaces them) but kept aside on the device, only to match your trades: the rebuilt history doesn't have every alert that went out (BTC's 1M L2 of 30 Oct is not in it). When nothing fits, the alert the trade was matched to (same symbol, side and day) still gives its setup, and a zone alert gives ZONE; when several alert types paired with it that day, the one with the closest entry. A journal trade with no stop also gets its matched alert's stop (so its R and risk work), and Analysis uses that same alert for a trade's channel, its alert stop and "No alert behind it". Only what was filled in automatically is changed later: a setup you picked, a level you ticked, or None stays. It runs when trades, alerts or the playbook change, and before a sync sends. The inspector shows "stop from alert" and "(from alert)". The scanner's fib and trendline alerts start on 29 Sep 2026, so older trades can't be placed.
- **Signals**: imported alerts by day, setup, score, outcome, symbol, channel, sector, timeframe and hour, with R and % move. Alerts stay on each device. The journal reads the scanner's files by itself when it opens, when Signals opens, when its tab comes back into view and every 10 minutes while it stays open (a failed read is tried again 2 minutes later, and the line under the title says so; new alerts wait to redraw while you're typing; from a week before the newest alert the device has, or further back to the oldest fib, trendline or 1D+ zone alert still open, up to 90 days, so a 1W or 1M result that closes weeks later still arrives) and adds new alerts and results, so recent trades always have alerts to match. Fib, trendline and 1D/1W/1M S/R zone results are priced like the scanner's report (out at +1R, or +2R when it hits 2R; Delta's 0.0826% fee for crypto, 0.1063% for NSE, the breakeven offset and the 0.05% stop slippage), and an alert already loaded is re-priced when that changes. 1D+ zones show under Zone alerts. **30m S/R zone alerts are not loaded or kept** (the scanner turned them off on 16 Oct 2026). Zone alerts from 1 Sep 2026 up to the rebuild's cutoff come from the scanner's **rebuilt zone backtest** (`zone_rebuild_*` files: every 4H / 1D / 1W / 1M zone alert today's rules would have sent, graded by today's scoring, fills within 15 min of US CPI / PPI / FOMC left out); zone alerts graded under older rules are dropped from the device when a rebuild arrives, and live zone alerts after the cutoff load as before. Fib and trendline alerts work the same way with the scanner's **rebuilt fib/trendline backtest** (`fibtl_rebuild_*`: every fib level and trendline touch today's rules would have sent since **1 Jun 2026**, Sep 1 until 7 Nov 2026). The rebuilt files are several MB, so a device downloads them once per rebuild (and once per app version); later refreshes read only their small meta files and skip them while the rebuild is unchanged. **Load scanner alerts** is still there to load a longer range by hand. The line under the Signals title shows the newest alert and when it last checked. Under **Fib alerts**, **Level** (All · L1 · L2 · L3 · L4, worked out from each alert's fib) backtests one level: every fib alert as if it entered there, with its own stop. An alert that entered at that level keeps its result; one that entered at another level takes the scanner's daily what-if for it (`fib_level_results.json`, marked *what-if Lx* in the table; not reached = no fill); alerts with neither are left out. The scanner enters fibs at L1 or L3, so L2 and L4 are all what-ifs. Every number on the tab follows it (headline, calendar, Backtest report, Edge & breakdowns, Exit at, Fees); it is hidden for Zone and Trendline alerts. Under **Trendline alerts**, **Type** (All · Support TL · Resistance TL · SP TL BR · RS TL BR) keeps one kind of trendline alert: longs at the lower line, shorts at the upper line, shorts through the lower line or longs through the upper line (line from the alert key, plus side). Everything on the tab follows it. The scanner does not send break alerts yet, so the two break types stay empty until it does. Your trades are held against the alerts: journal trades plus CoinDCX trades, whose side comes from the fills, or from the fees when the fills haven't come in yet (a trade from today). **My trades with no alert** lists every one in the period (paged only past 50). A **Backtest report** section plays every alert with a result in order, as if each was taken at 1R: win rate, expectancy, profit factor, average win and loss, payoff ratio, max drawdown and how long it lasted, recovery factor, win and loss streaks, spread (SD) and SQN, fill rate and alerts a day; an equity curve with its drawdown; the spread of results and how they ended (+2R, +1R, BE, SL); **What to cut** (groups of 5+ alerts that lost R, and what the rest made without them); **If you skip thin stops** (the same numbers keeping only stops at least 0.2% to 1% wide); and a breakdown by channel, symbol, side, stop width, score, hour, weekday, week, month or BTC day, sortable by any column. **How sure is this?** under the headline figures: the sample (too few under 30, early read under 100, fair, solid from 300), the 95% range of the average R (mean ± t × SD ÷ √n, t ≈ 2 and wider on a small sample: above zero, below zero or could still be zero) and of the win rate (Wilson, against the break-even win rate at this payoff), the first half vs the second half of the alerts in time order, months and weeks in profit, **time in trade** (median and average, fill to close, from the scanner's own fill and close times; with Exit at Scanner only) and trades **still open** (not counted; with a fixed Exit at, where they stand at the last price read). **Top coins vs all crypto** (lakky, 2026-11-07) splits the same alerts, before the Coins filter, into All crypto, Top 3, Top 5, Other coins (not top 5) and Your picks, then each top coin: alerts, graded, win %, average R with its 95% range, total R, profit factor, max drawdown and the longest losing run; under it, Top 3 / Top 5 / picks vs the rest with the gap in average R and whether it is *likely real* (Welch t ≥ 2, each side 10+ results), *maybe* (t ≥ 1) or *could be luck*. **Export CSV** saves the alerts in the view with their results. It follows the period, channel, coins and BTC-day filters. **Coins** (All · Top 3 · Top 5 · Picks) keeps only those coins' alerts everywhere on the tab: Top 3 / Top 5 by market cap (BTC, ETH, XRP, BNB, SOL; stablecoins left out) or by the most alerts in view, Picks = coins you tick (BTC, ETH, SOL to start, each with its alert count). Gold, silver, US stocks and NSE are not coins, so they are left out while it is on; with no crypto in view it is off. **Exit at** (Scanner · 1R · 1.5R · 2R · 3R · 1% · 2% · 3%) re-scores every graded alert as if it closed at that one fixed target, with its own stop and no breakeven move: the headline numbers, calendar, Edge & breakdowns (By outcome then shows Target / SL / Time exit) and Alert vs my trade all follow it. Crypto prices come from Delta's public candles. NSE alerts use `nse-paths.json` in the sync repo, written twice a day by `tools/nse-paths.mjs` (run by the sync repo's `nse-paths.yml` Action), which reads Yahoo's candles and walks them the same way over 20 NSE candles of the alert's timeframe (4H = 09:15-13:15 and 13:15-15:30); the journal reads it with the sync token, so NSE needs GitHub sync on. NSE fees are 0.1063%. Each alert is read from its alert time until 20 candles of its own timeframe after the fill; a candle touching both the stop and the target counts as the stop, and a target not hit by then closes at that candle's close (Time exit). Costs as the scanner: 0.0826% round trip and 0.05% stop slippage. Finished alerts' candles are read once and kept on the device. **Move SL** (Off · BE · SL shift) moves the stop on top of any fixed Exit at: **BE** puts it at entry once price reaches the trigger (+0.5R to +3R, 1R by default); **SL shift** puts it at a level you pick once price reaches the trigger (e.g. at +1R lock +0.75R; levels from -0.5R up to just under the trigger). With an R target, triggers at or above it are greyed out (the target comes first, so the stop would never move) and a saved one past it is flagged in amber; With a % target (1% / 2% / 3%) the trigger and the level are % moves from entry too (at +0.25% to +3%, to -0.25% up to just under the trigger; 1% → +0.5% by default), and the same greying applies. **Trail TP** (Off · On) lets a fixed target run: each time price reaches the target, the target moves up a step (+0.25R / +0.5R / +1R) and the stop goes to a gap behind the target just reached (at the TP, 0.25R, 0.5R, 0.75R or 1R behind) but never below the first target (once hit, the TP is locked), from the next candle; e.g. target 1.5R, +0.25R, SL 0.5R behind: at 1.5R the stop goes to +1.5R, at 1.75R and 2R it stays there, at 2.25R it goes to +1.75R, and so on until the stop is hit (By outcome: Trail TP) or Hold ends. With a % target the steps and gaps are % moves. Move SL works up to the target (its triggers at or past the target stay greyed out and never fire); after the target Trail TP moves the stop, and where both apply the higher stop counts. The compare tables apply Trail TP to every cell. The "till TP / SL" walk goes on past the biggest target (3R / 3%) until price falls back 2R / 2% from its best on a later candle, so every Trail TP stop is settled (summaries kept before it, without `tr`, are read once more; `tools/nse-paths.mjs` does the same). **Hold** (Till TP / SL · 20 candles) sets how long a fixed-target trade stays open: **Till TP / SL** (the default) reads from the alert to now and keeps each trade until its target or its (moved) stop, however long it takes; one with neither yet has no result and the headline says how many are still open. **20 candles** is the scanner's window: closed at the 20th candle of the alert's timeframe after the fill (Time exit, under By outcome). Either way the fill must come within 20 candles of the alert. Each hold keeps its own walk (crypto: `tgtHoldKey`, the "till TP / SL" one under `<key>|tp`; NSE: `nse-paths.json` gets `<key>|tp` too, read up to now). The stop moves from the candle after the one that reached the trigger (the scanner's breakeven rule); a candle touching the moved stop and the target counts as the stop, and the moved stop slips 0.05% like any stop. Everything Exit at drives follows it (headline, calendar, breakdowns, By outcome shows BE / SL shift, Backtest report, Demo account). Scanner keeps the scanner's own rules. For this each alert's walk keeps a small price path (each new best move in R with the worst move before it, `tgtRec`; `pathRec` in `tools/nse-paths.mjs` for NSE); summaries kept before it are read once more. Edge & breakdowns adds **Move SL compared**: total R for every fixed target × no move, BE at 0.5R / 1R / 1.5R / 2R and shifts 1R→0.5R / 1R→0.75R / 1.5R→1R / 2R→1R / 2R→1.5R (and your own), on the same alerts, best underlined (with a % target: the % targets × BE / shifts in %); a cell whose trigger is at or past its R target reads *same* (same as no move); click a cell to use it. **Fees** (With / Without) shows every result on the tab with or without the round-trip fee: the scanner's 0.0826% (Delta and CoinDCX's 0.02% maker in + 0.05% taker out + 18% GST; NSE 0.1063%), added back in R as fee % ÷ stop %. Headline numbers, calendar, Exit targets compared, every breakdown, the Backtest report follow it; stop slippage counts either way. Edge & breakdowns opens with **Exit targets compared**: the same alerts at every target side by side (win rate, total and average R, profit factor, total %), best marked; click a row to switch the tab to it. Under it, the breakdown cards (outcome, channel, timeframe, sector or market, stop distance, score, symbol, half hour) stack in up to three balanced columns so a short card leaves no gap; each group is one row (alerts, won, total R, a bar, average R and %; narrow cards drop the bar, then the total; hover a row for every figure). **By symbol** lists the 6 best and 6 worst by total R, with **Show more** for the rest; a breakdown where every alert falls in one group folds into a *Same for all* line. **Demo account** plays the same alerts as a pretend account: pick a starting balance ($100 by default, in $ or ₹), risk per trade (a % of the balance, 1% by default, or a fixed amount) and an optional leverage cap (No cap, 2x-100x). Every alert with a result in the view is taken one after another in the order it fired, compounding: each trade risks that share of the balance at that point and makes or loses risk × its R before fees (so it follows Exit at, Type / Level, channel, period and BTC day), less its fee. **Fees: trades on** Delta / CoinDCX / No fees picks the broker: each trade pays position × (entry leg + exit leg) × 1.18 (18% GST), position = risk ÷ stop distance. **Orders** picks the legs: limit in, market out (maker + taker, the scanner's way and the default), market in and out (taker + taker) or limit in and out (maker + maker); the maker and taker rates are editable per broker (both 0.02% / 0.05%, as lakky's own fills show, so by default 0.0826% a trade, the same as before). NSE alerts pay 0.1063% whatever the broker. **No fees** is the tab's Fees switch (Without), so the two always agree. A line above the numbers says Fees on or off, the broker, orders and rate, the total paid and the balance the other way (without fees, or with them when off, with a **Charge fees** button); each trade shows its fee, and its Result is in R after it. It shows start, final balance and peak, return, trades and wins, max drawdown (% from a high, and the most in money), lowest balance, best / worst trade, and the most leverage any trade needed (position = risk ÷ stop distance; with a cap, a trade that needs more is taken at the cap and risks less); the balance curve with its drawdown; progress by day, week or month (by how long the view is); the same alerts at 0.5% / 1% / 2% / 3% / 5% risk; and every trade, newest first (risk, position and leverage, P&L, balance after). **Real orders** (On by default; lakky, 2026-11-07) places each trade the way the broker would take it, so a stop that can't really be traded at your size isn't counted as if it could: the position is rounded **down** to whole contracts (Delta's contract size for that coin, e.g. BTC 0.001, ETH 0.01, SOL 1; CoinDCX's quantity step and minimum order), so a trade never risks more than planned; leverage is capped at the coin's own maximum on that broker (Delta: BTC/ETH 200x, majors 100x, most alts 20x, some 10x; plus your cap if lower); a stop under the **Min stop** (0.10% by default) or within 3 ticks is skipped; a coin the broker doesn't list is skipped. **Order size** (lakky, 2026-11-08): **Max within risk** (the default) takes as many whole contracts as the risk allows and skips a trade where even 1 risks more; **Take 1 if too small** takes 1 contract instead, but only while it risks at most 1.5× / 2× (default) / 3× / 5× the plan, or whatever it risks (the line says the most of the balance one trade then risked; one over the limit is skipped and says so); **Always 1 contract (test)** makes every trade exactly 1 contract (CoinDCX: 1 min order), whatever it risks: risk per trade is not used and the other-risk-sizes table is hidden. Skipped trades stay in the trade list with the reason (e.g. *1 contract (0.001 BTC) = $75.00 risks $3.00 at this stop, more than the $2.00 planned*); each placed trade shows its **Size** in coins. A blue line says how many were placed and skipped and why, and the result at any size (**Off (any size)** is the old way, with the real-orders result in its line). Specs are Delta's and CoinDCX's public APIs read on 7 Nov 2026 (`research/demo_sl_audit.py` in the scanner repo); Delta's list is read again live when the section opens. Rupee accounts convert at ₹85 a dollar (Delta) or ₹99.30 (CoinDCX). NSE alerts: 1 share, 5x. US stock alerts (MRVL, MSFT, …) map to Delta's bStocks (MRVLBUSD), for real orders and for Exit at's candles. An account can't lose more than it holds: one that hits 0 says on which trade and stops there. Wrong inputs (empty, 0, negative, over 100%, too large) say what is wrong. The settings are kept on the device. To keep the tab quick, the heavier sections (Backtest report, Demo account, Edge & breakdowns, Market days) are drawn only when open, and each alert's result is worked out once per redraw.
- **Analysis**: deeper breakdowns, including Market days (BTC daily calendar, and hour by hour for a clicked day).
- **AI Trade Review** (Analysis tab): pick This month (default), Last month, 7 days or 30 days and press **Review my trades**. Every book's closed trades for the period (Delta, Dhan and CoinDCX report trades) go to Google Gemini with your book limits, playbook, totals by setup, level, side, symbol, hour and rule flag, and one line per trade (entry/exit, size × balance, P&L, R, stop, setup and fib level, tags, rules kept, flags, notes; the latest 400 at most). Before asking, the journal reads Delta's public candles (no key) around every crypto trade with prices (Delta trades, and CoinDCX trades whose fills give entry and exit; the symbol becomes Delta's, e.g. LINK/USDT → LINKUSD) and works out, per trade: the best and worst move while open (mfe / mae, in % and in R, or in 0.5% units with no stop), the share of the best move kept, minutes from the best price to the exit, and the best and worst move in the 1 h and 4 h after the exit. Each trade is labelled early_exit, cut_then_recovered, gave_back, held_loser or good_exit, with counts and the ₹ left by early exits, plus whether it came after a win or a loss that day. Finished trades' measures are kept on the device (`tapeAndTarget.aiPx`). It also adds a **Trailing SL** block (trades tagged trailed / moved SL / not tagged: wins, net, % of the best move kept, early exits, your R vs the VAS alert's own R on the same trades) and a **VAS alerts** block (fib, trendline and zone alerts sent in the period, taken vs skipped, by type, timeframe, side, score, scanner outcome and hour: alert R vs your R), so the AI can judge whether your TradingView check adds value and what to change in VAS. Each trade line names its alert, alert R, your R and stop handling. Gemini is told to go through every trade and alert and answers in short bullets (point ≤ 12 words, proof with numbers): **Verdict**, **What you do wrong**, **What works**, **Trailing SL**, **VAS alerts**, **Setup**, **Do this next**; the last review stays on screen on that device. Needs a free Gemini API key from aistudio.google.com, pasted in Settings → Connections → **AI Review (Gemini)**; once saved the card shows only **Key saved** with **Change key** (empty boxes for a new one) and **Remove key** (model defaults to `gemini-2.5-flash`; when Gemini is busy (503) each model is retried after 3 s and 8 s, then `gemini-flash-latest`, `gemini-flash-lite-latest` and `gemini-2.5-flash-lite` take over; a missing model or the free limit moves straight to the next, and when any model was busy the message says Gemini is overloaded; a dropped connection ("Failed to fetch") first checks Gemini can be reached at all: if not, it stops and says so (offline, or turn off VPN / ad-blocker / DNS filter, or switch network), else the same model is tried once more, then the next; a call with no answer in 150 s moves to the next model; after 3 dropped calls it stops with "kept dropping"). **Copy for Gemini app** copies the same text to paste into the Gemini app with no key.
- **Broker reports**: upload an Excel (.xlsx) or CSV trade report, such as a CoinDCX futures report. The broker is read from the file (and can be changed before import) and gets a book of its own, next to Dhan and Delta. Its closed trades count everywhere a journal trade does: dashboard P&L, calendar, equity curve, goals and the book's balance, dated by the day they closed. Analysis shows them trade by trade (grouped from the report rows, with fees, funding, an equity curve and breakdowns by pair and day), with a "Results by broker" table to compare books. A broker book's **Size & mistakes** section runs the same size and mistake analysis as a journal book (rule scorecard, oversized trades, loss over limit, revenge and size-up after a loss, daily limits, fees that ate a win, position-size chart, trade by trade). A report has no prices, but CoinDCX's fee is a fixed share of each order's value (0.02% maker / 0.05% taker + 18% GST; 0.02% / 0.1% before Feb 2025; 0.01% + GST on gold and silver), so each trade's side and position value are worked out from its fees: the rates and side whose values add up to the report's gross P&L. Trades whose fees don't add up use the fills' prices, or show — for size. Balances before each trade are anchored to the CoinDCX wallet, as Delta's are to its wallet figure. The side also shows in the ledger and inspector. Its entry and exit (from the fills, once they come in) show in Day's trades and the All trades list too. Each report trade opens in the same Trade Inspector as a journal trade (from the Dashboard ledger, Signals, or the **Review** button in that table), with side and prices when the broker's fills give them; its review (rules kept, what went right, mistakes, notes, confidence) is kept with the journal (`repReviews`, synced), apart from the report's own numbers. Uploading the same report twice adds nothing twice. Uploaded files (and the CoinDCX API import) are listed, removed and uploaded in Settings → Connections → **Broker Report Files**, by broker.
- **Calculator**: position size and risk for NSE (INR) and crypto (USD) accounts. A linked book sizes from the same balance Trading Books shows (Delta and CoinDCX wallets). **Quick size**: type a stop-loss and the risk to take; it shows the **capital used** (margin, your own money) and the **position size** at the leverage (₹1,000 at 5× = ₹5,000 position), plus the loss if the stop hits. The risk follows the header ₹ / $ switch: type ₹150, switch to $ and it becomes $150 ÷ the book's USD/INR rate, with every size shown in dollars.
- **Nifty options** (Calculator tab): for a Nifty idea from your chart (a fib entry and stop), which option to buy. Type the **entry** and **stop-loss** (and a target, or pick 1R / 1.5R / 2R / 3R); a stop below the entry is a **CALL (CE)**, above it a **PUT (PE)**. It names the contract (e.g. **BUY NIFTY 25,600 CE · Tue 17 Nov**) and the lots: the smaller of what your **account size** pays for (a bought option costs its whole premium; typed, else the NSE book's balance) and what your **risk** allows (typed in ₹, else a % of the account picked next to it, 0.5% to 10%, starting at the book's max risk). Each strike shows its cost a lot and how many lots the account pays for. When the account can't pay for one lot near the money it names the nearest strike further out that it can (delta 0.15 at least, marked far OTM), or says it can't. It also shows what you pay, the loss at the stop and the profit at the target with charges in, and the reward : risk. Every strike from ITM 2 to OTM 3 is listed with its premium, delta, lots and results; tap one to pick it. The automatic pick is the strike that makes the most at the target for your risk among those that follow Nifty well (delta 0.35-0.75); when even 1 lot is over your risk it says so instead of BUY, with the account that 1 lot would take at your risk %. **Expiry**: the first one far enough out for the chart (intraday 1 day, 4H 5, 1D 10, 1W 30 days, and at least 3× the expected hold), from Tuesday weeklies and last-Tuesday monthlies; an NSE holiday Tuesday expires the day before (10 Nov and 24 Nov 2026 expire on Monday). Premiums are Black-Scholes estimates from **India VIX** (14% unless typed); type the **premium your app shows** for the named strike (and Nifty at that moment) and every price is worked out from its volatility instead. The stop is priced at once (a stop on the premium caps the loss there), the target after the expected hold's time decay. Also shows the time decay a day per lot and how soon it alone would reach the stop. Lot 65, strikes every 50; lot size and brokerage (₹20 an order) can be changed; charges are STT 0.15% of the sell, exchange 0.03553%, SEBI, stamp 0.003% of the buy and 18% GST. Option prices are always in ₹; with the header on $ a line gives the dollar amounts. Holidays are known to the end of 2026; later expiries carry a reminder to check the date.
- **Goal planner** (Calculator tab): type a target, the number of days and your trades per day; it shows what each day and each trade must make and the trades in all (₹600 in 2 days at 3 trades a day = ₹300 a day, ₹100 a trade, 6 trades). The target can be typed as an amount or as a **% of capital** (e.g. 10% of the balance); the box shows the other one under it, and switching converts what you typed. Pick a goal instead and it fills in what is still to earn and the trading days left. The win rate, reward : risk and position-size check sits folded under **Advanced**. The planner, like the whole calculator, follows the header's ₹ INR / $ USD switch; goals, balances and a linked book are converted to it.
- **Header**: stays on one line from tablet width up; on narrower screens the subtitle and "Balance:" word drop out and the book switcher scrolls sideways. The version pill stays on every width.
- **What's new**: tap the version pill next to the title to see every update, newest first: version, title, date, PR number and short bullets, with this build marked **Current**. Close, Escape or a tap outside shuts it. The list is `CHANGELOG` in `index.html`.
- **Goal profit as a % of capital**: in the goal editor (+ Add / edit on the goal card), the Profit target has a ₹ / **% of capital** picker. Pick %, type e.g. 10, and the goal aims for 10% of the books' balance when its window opened (a weekly goal: the balance on Monday), worked out again each new week or month as the capital grows or shrinks. The editor shows the amount under the box, and the card shows "· 10% of capital" next to the target. Switching back to ₹ turns the % into today's amount. Saved on the goal as `pnlPct` and synced.
- **Withdrawals**: the **Withdraw** button on the goal card logs money taken out to your bank (book, date, amount, note), and lists or removes past ones. A goal can have a **Money withdrawn (banked)** target, so it only counts as done once the profit is actually taken out. The date starts on today's trading day (it rolls over at the day cutoff), future dates are refused, and an amount bigger than the book's balance asks first. Withdrawals lower a book's worked-out balance (a live Delta or CoinDCX wallet already shows them) and sync like trades (`withdrawals` in `journal.json`).
- **Trade inspector**: click any trade, or its **Review** button in Analysis, to open it; tap a rule, setup-checklist item or tag to switch it on or off (saved at once). Pick the setup from the playbook's names (or None) in one tap; its checklist shows even before anything on it is ticked. For a levels setup (FIB, TRENDLINE) the list is its levels: ○ marks the ones it was not at, and tapping one makes it the only one.
- **Setup playbook** (Settings → Books & rules): each setup's rules are a checklist or, with **These are levels, not a checklist** ticked (FIB and TRENDLINE are by default), levels a trade is at one of. A checklist setup compares trades that kept every rule with ones that broke one; a levels setup shows each level's trades, win rate and P&L, and how many have no level. A line on top says how many trades have a setup, a level, and how many were filled in from alerts.
- **Settings**: books and rules, connections (Delta Exchange import, CoinDCX import, GitHub sync, Gemini key, report files), CSV export and backups. Trades come in from Delta and CoinDCX, so logging by hand is the small **+ Log trade by hand** link on the Trading Books card; the mobile bottom bar has no + button. A book with nothing in it (no trades, starting balance or withdrawals, like an unused Dhan book) is left off the book bar; Trading Books still lists it, marked "hidden from the bar (empty)".
- **CoinDCX API import**: CoinDCX doesn't answer web pages, so GitHub reads it for you. The sync repo runs `.github/workflows/coindcx.yml` every hour, which runs `tools/coindcx-import.mjs` from this repo with the key in the sync repo's secrets (`COINDCX_API_KEY`, `COINDCX_API_SECRET`). It adds new futures transactions (INR and USDT margined) to `journal.json`; each device gets them with its next sync, in the CoinDCX book an uploaded report fills. A transaction that is also in an uploaded report counts once (same pair and kind, within 3 seconds, same net). It also reads the fills (price, side and size) a week at a time, the first week ending tomorrow: CoinDCX stops at the start of the end day, so ending at today left today's fills out. GitHub skips many scheduled runs (3 to 6 hours apart on some days), so while the journal is open it starts the import itself once the last run is an hour old (checked through the sync token, which then needs **Actions: Read and write** as well as Contents), and syncs a minute later to pick up the new trades and wallet balance; without that permission the card says so and GitHub's own schedule (twice an hour) still runs it. A failed run says why on Settings → Connections → CoinDCX card. **Import now** on that card (or Signals → More → Import CoinDCX now) starts a run from any device, the phone too, then watches it every 10 seconds for 15 minutes: it says whether the run is waiting for the PC, running or done, and syncs once it is done. CoinDCX has no read-only keys, so use a separate key. Since 2026-10-29 CoinDCX locks every key to one IP address, so the workflow runs on lakky's PC (a self-hosted runner, label `coindcx`), and the key is made for that PC's public IP. A run waits in GitHub's queue while the PC is off. When CoinDCX refuses the key (401/403), the script reads the PC's IP from api.ipify.org (`IP_URL`) and puts it in the reason the card shows, so a new key can be made for it. While runs fail, the CoinDCX books keep the last wallet read: the header tag turns amber **LAST KNOWN** (not **WALLET**), Trading Books says "last known (import failing)" and the card says it is from before the failure, so a deposit or transfer since then is not in it until a run works again.
- **Trading day (05:30)**: the day rolls over at 05:30 IST (00:00 UTC, when exchanges reset their day), not midnight, so a trade at 01:30 counts for the day before on the dashboard, calendar, equity curve, daily loss and trade limits, goals, weekly review and Analysis (journal and broker-report trades alike). The trade keeps its real date and time; only the day it counts toward moves. Change it (midnight to 07:00) on the Settings → Books & rules → **Trading Day** card; it is saved on that device. An hour picked before (other than the old 2 AM default) is kept. The Signals tab and BTC market days stay on calendar days.
- **Rupees and dollars**: the ₹ INR / $ USD switch at the start of the accounts bar shows every book, tab and figure (P&L, fees, balances, limits, goals, broker reports) in one currency. Each book converts at its own rate: dollar books at Delta India's fixed ₹85, CoinDCX at its USDT price (₹99.30 by default, from its wallet ₹ ÷ $). Change a rate in Settings → Books & rules → Trading Books → Edit. Prices and sizes stay as traded; the trade inspector shows the book's own figure next to the converted one. Because the rates differ, an All accounts total in ₹ is not the $ total × one rate: a Delta loss and a CoinDCX win of the same dollars net to about zero in $ but to a profit in ₹ (the CoinDCX dollar is worth more rupees), so P&L, profit factor and return % can differ a little between the two. Set both rates the same to make them match. A goal's % of capital profit is taken from the capital in the currency shown, and the Today card's max risk on All accounts shows every book's (a range when they differ).

## Input checks

Typed values are checked before they are saved, and the same rules run on anything loaded from a backup or GitHub:

- A trade needs entry, exit and quantity above 0, and price × quantity under 10^15 (bigger is a typo that would blank every total).
- Trade and withdrawal dates run from 2000 to today. Symbols keep 30 letters.
- A goal's ticked target must be a number above 0. A book's max risk is 100% at most, and max trades a day at least 1.

## Where data lives

- **This browser**: the journal is saved in `localStorage` (`tapeAndTarget.v2`; `tapeAndTarget.mktHourly` for cached BTC candles). Alerts are kept in IndexedDB (database `tapeAndTarget`, key `tapeAndTarget.signals`), which holds far more than localStorage's ~5 MB, so a long alert history can no longer fill the space trades need. Alerts an older build left in localStorage move to IndexedDB on the next open; a browser without IndexedDB keeps them in localStorage as before. The candle summaries behind Signals' Exit at / Move SL / Trail TP live in the same IndexedDB store (key `tapeAndTarget.sigTgt`; an older build's localStorage copy moves across, and without IndexedDB they stay in localStorage only while under 1 MB). Offline, no prices are read (Signals says so and reads them when the connection is back); with Delta unreachable the reads stop after a dozen failures and Retry picks them up. The storage line in Settings → Backup counts localStorage only and shows the alerts' size beside it. Clearing site data clears the journal.
- **Broker report uploads**: stored with the journal (`backtest` in `tapeAndTarget.v2` and in `journal.json` once something is uploaded): the report's rows only, never the cover sheet's name, email or PAN. Each uploaded file keeps the broker it belongs to; the broker's book (`rep-<broker>-<currency>`) is made from it on any device. The file is read in the browser; nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Deleted records**: a deletion leaves a marker so sync doesn't bring the record back from another device. Markers are dropped after 30 days (183 for a deleted Delta trade's order tag, which stops the Delta import re-adding it), so `journal.json` stays small. A device not synced for over 30 days could bring back something deleted since.
- **GitHub sync (optional)**: in Settings → Connections, add your sync repo (`owner/repo`) and a fine-grained token with *Contents: Read and write* on it. The journal is saved there as `journal.json` on the `main` branch (there is no Branch field; a setup saved on another branch by an older build keeps it), so other devices can pick it up. Both fields show as dots, with plain hints, so the screen never shows the repo name or token. The token and webhook never leave the browser; the Gemini key only inside `keysLock` once you turn that on (next point); the Delta and CoinDCX keys live only in the sync repo's Actions secrets, for the PC's imports.
- **Keys on your other devices** (in the GitHub sync card): each browser keeps its own storage (on an iPhone the home-screen app doesn't share Safari's), so a new device used to have no Gemini key. **Save keys to GitHub** asks for a passphrase (8+ letters, typed twice) and saves the Gemini key in `journal.json` as `keysLock`: AES-256-GCM under a key made from the passphrase with PBKDF2-SHA256 (600,000 rounds, random salt), so GitHub only holds the sealed copy; the passphrase is never saved or sent. A device that connects GitHub and finds the lock asks for the passphrase once (or unlock it in the card) and fills the keys in. It keeps the derived key in its own sync settings (never synced), so a key saved later on any device fills in on the others at their next sync: a key that changed in the lock is filled in where that device's copy is empty or still the old one; a key you changed or removed on one device stays as you left it there. **Set up another device** (there in the Off state too: it asks for the passphrase first) shows a setup code, a link (`#vjsetup=…`, taken out of the address bar at once) and its QR code: the repo and token sealed the same way. Paste the code in **Use setup code** (or in Repository Name) with the passphrase and GitHub connects and the keys fill in; on an iPhone, copy it in Safari and paste it in the home-screen app. A code for a different repo than the device uses asks first. **Forgot it? Start over** replaces the lock with this device's keys under a new passphrase (other devices keep theirs and ask for the new one). **Take keys off GitHub** leaves an `off` marker; every device keeps the keys it has. Disconnect forgets the passphrase key on that device. Backup files never include `keysLock`. Delta and CoinDCX need nothing here: their keys are in the sync repo's Actions secrets. A lock an older build made with the Delta key inside is resealed without it by the first device that opens it.
- **Delta balance across devices**: the PC's Delta import writes Delta's wallet figure to `journal.json` as `deltaBalance` (when it moves, or hourly), and every device shows it instead of its own trade sum. The header tag says WALLET while the figure is under 2 hours old and LAST KNOWN after that (PC off); its tooltip says when your PC's import read it. No browser fetches it with a key any more.
- **Delta through the PC** (the only Delta import since v2026-11-03.151; the Delta card has no key boxes, Test & Connect or in-page Import Now / Full History): Delta only answers a key from the IPs whitelisted on it, and a phone's IP (Jio, IPv4 and IPv6) keeps changing, so an import in the browser failed there with `ip_not_whitelisted_for_api_key`. The sync repo's `delta.yml` Action runs `tools/delta-import.mjs` on lakky's PC (the self-hosted runner that runs the CoinDCX import, label `coindcx`, IPv4 only) with a Read Data key whitelisted for the PC's public IP, kept in the sync repo's secrets (`DELTA_API_KEY`, `DELTA_API_SECRET`). It rebuilds trades from the fills (as the old in-page import did), adds the new ones to `journal.json`, keeps where it left off as `deltaImport` (newest fill read, positions still open, the reason a run failed) and writes the wallet figure to `deltaBalance`. Each trade carries its Delta order tag, so a trade either import brought in, or one deleted (its tag's tombstone), is never added twice; the first run reads the whole history once. A refused IP names the IP Delta saw (or the PC's, from api.ipify.org). The Delta card shows its state (Not set up / Importing / Last run failed) and the steps; a run GitHub calls a success that wrote nothing (no secrets yet) says Delta isn't set up, not "nothing new". A Delta key an older build saved on the device shows **Copy key** / **Copy secret** for the two secrets, and is deleted from the device by itself once the PC import has worked (or with **Delete it**). **Undo Delta Trades** removes them and makes the PC's next run read the whole history again. **Import now** (CoinDCX card, Delta card, Signals → More → Import CoinDCX + Delta) starts both jobs from any device, watches each and syncs as each finishes; Delta's job missing from the sync repo just means it isn't set up. While the journal is open it also starts Delta's job when the last run is an hour old, as for CoinDCX.
- **Gemini key**: kept in this browser (`tapeAndTarget.gemini`), outside the journal, so it is never put in backups and reaches `journal.json` only inside `keysLock`, when Keys on your other devices is on; the last AI review is kept in `tapeAndTarget.aiReview`. A review sends that period's trades (never any key or token) to Google.
- **Export**: Settings → Backup can download trades as CSV.

## Updating

Replace `index.html` and bump `<meta name="app-version">` near the top, so the version pill in the header shows which build each device is on. Add an entry for the new version at the top of `CHANGELOG` (same version, a short title, 1-3 bullets); the smoke test fails if the newest entry doesn't match the meta version.
Chart.js loads from jsDelivr; without it, charts are skipped and the rest still works.
