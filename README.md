# Victus Journal

An intraday trading journal and workstation in a single file, `index.html`. No build step and no server: open the file in a browser (desktop or phone) and it runs.

## Tabs

- **Dashboard**: P&L, goals, time-of-day, confidence and mistake/positive tags. Its **Review** tab (with the count waiting, e.g. "Review (5)") lists every trade from the last 30 days not reviewed yet (no tag, rule, notes, confidence or Clean mark), journal and CoinDCX alike: tap **✓ Clean** if there's nothing to note, a mistake (FOMO Entry, Early Exit, Moved Stop Loss, Revenge Trade) to tag it, or **Review** to open the inspector. Each tap takes the trade off the list. The week summary (P&L, win rate, best setup, costliest mistake, P&L by day) sits under it.
- **Stop and setup from the alert**: a trade matched to a scanner alert gets the alert's stop when it has none (so its R and risk work), its setup (FIB, TRENDLINE or ZONE, or the playbook setup of that name; fib and trendline win over a zone on the same trade) and, for a fib, the level its entry was nearest (L1-L4) ticked in that setup's checklist. Only empty fields are filled; a typed stop, setup or ticked checklist is never changed. CoinDCX trades get the setup and level in their review. It runs when trades, alerts or the playbook change, and before a sync sends. The inspector shows "stop from alert" and "(from alert)". This replaces the old **Fill stops from signals** button.
- **Signals**: imported alerts by day, setup, score, outcome, symbol, channel, sector, timeframe and hour, with R and % move. Alerts stay on each device. The journal reads the scanner's files by itself when it opens and when Signals opens (at most every 15 minutes, from a week before the newest alert the device has, or further back to the oldest fib, trendline or 1D+ zone alert still open, up to 90 days, so a 1W or 1M result that closes weeks later still arrives) and adds new alerts and results, so recent trades always have alerts to match. Fib, trendline and 1D/1W/1M S/R zone results are priced like the scanner's report (out at +1R, or +2R when it hits 2R; Delta's 0.0826% fee for crypto, 0.1063% for NSE, the breakeven offset and the 0.05% stop slippage), and an alert already loaded is re-priced when that changes. 1D+ zones show under Zone alerts. **Load scanner alerts** is still there to load a longer range by hand. The line under the Signals title shows the newest alert and when it last checked. Under **Fib alerts**, a **Fib levels** section breaks the alerts down by level (L1-L4, worked out from each alert's fib): how many reached each level, alerts entering there, filled, hit +1R, win rate, total and average R, profit factor, best and worst; the same by how far price was from the entry when the alert fired; and your own trades on those alerts by the level your entry was nearest. The scanner enters fibs at L3 only, so L1, L2 and L4 show its daily what-if (`fib_level_results.json`): the same alerts as if entered at that level, with the same stop. It follows the period, channel and BTC-day filters. Your trades are held against the alerts: journal trades plus CoinDCX trades, whose side comes from the fills, or from the fees when the fills haven't come in yet (a trade from today). **My trades with no alert** lists every one in the period (paged only past 50). A **Backtest report** section plays every alert with a result in order, as if each was taken at 1R: win rate, expectancy, profit factor, average win and loss, payoff ratio, max drawdown and how long it lasted, recovery factor, win and loss streaks, spread (SD) and SQN, fill rate and alerts a day; an equity curve with its drawdown; the spread of results and how they ended (+2R, +1R, BE, SL); **What to cut** (groups of 5+ alerts that lost R, and what the rest made without them); **If you skip thin stops** (the same numbers keeping only stops at least 0.2% to 1% wide); and a breakdown by channel, symbol, side, stop width, score, hour, weekday, week, month or BTC day, sortable by any column. **Export CSV** saves the alerts in the view with their results. It follows the period, channel and BTC-day filters. To keep the tab quick, the heavier sections (Backtest report, Edge & breakdowns, Fib levels, Market days) are drawn only when open, and each alert's result is worked out once per redraw.
- **Analysis**: deeper breakdowns, including Market days (BTC daily calendar, and hour by hour for a clicked day).
- **Broker reports**: upload an Excel (.xlsx) or CSV trade report, such as a CoinDCX futures report. The broker is read from the file (and can be changed before import) and gets a book of its own, next to Dhan and Delta. Its closed trades count everywhere a journal trade does: dashboard P&L, calendar, equity curve, goals and the book's balance, dated by the day they closed. Analysis shows them trade by trade (grouped from the report rows, with fees, funding, an equity curve and breakdowns by pair and day), with a "Results by broker" table to compare books. A broker book's **Size & mistakes** section runs the same size and mistake analysis as a journal book (rule scorecard, oversized trades, loss over limit, revenge and size-up after a loss, daily limits, fees that ate a win, position-size chart, trade by trade). A report has no prices, but CoinDCX's fee is a fixed share of each order's value (0.02% maker / 0.05% taker + 18% GST; 0.02% / 0.1% before Feb 2025; 0.01% + GST on gold and silver), so each trade's side and position value are worked out from its fees: the rates and side whose values add up to the report's gross P&L. Trades whose fees don't add up use the fills' prices, or show — for size. Balances before each trade are anchored to the CoinDCX wallet, as Delta's are to its live balance. The side also shows in the ledger and inspector. Each report trade opens in the same Trade Inspector as a journal trade (from the Dashboard ledger, Signals, or the **Review** button in that table), with side and prices when the broker's fills give them; its review (rules kept, what went right, mistakes, notes, confidence) is kept with the journal (`repReviews`, synced), apart from the report's own numbers. Uploading the same report twice adds nothing twice. Uploaded files (and the CoinDCX API import) are listed, removed and uploaded on the Sync & Settings tab's **Broker Report Files** card, by broker.
- **Calculator**: position size and risk for NSE (INR) and crypto (USD) accounts. A linked book sizes from the same balance Trading Books shows (Delta live, CoinDCX wallet). **Quick size**: type a stop-loss and the risk to take; it shows the **capital used** (margin, your own money) and the **position size** at the leverage (₹1,000 at 5× = ₹5,000 position), plus the loss if the stop hits. The risk follows the header ₹ / $ switch: type ₹150, switch to $ and it becomes $150 ÷ the book's USD/INR rate, with every size shown in dollars.
- **Goal planner** (Calculator tab): type a target, the number of days and your trades per day; it shows what each day and each trade must make and the trades in all (₹600 in 2 days at 3 trades a day = ₹300 a day, ₹100 a trade, 6 trades). The target can be typed as an amount or as a **% of capital** (e.g. 10% of the balance); the box shows the other one under it, and switching converts what you typed. Pick a goal instead and it fills in what is still to earn and the trading days left. The win rate, reward : risk and position-size check sits folded under **Advanced**. The planner, like the whole calculator, follows the header's ₹ INR / $ USD switch; goals, balances and a linked book are converted to it.
- **Header**: stays on one line from tablet width up; on narrower screens the subtitle, version and "Balance:" word drop out and the book switcher scrolls sideways.
- **Goal profit as a % of capital**: in the goal editor (+ Add / edit on the goal card), the Profit target has a ₹ / **% of capital** picker. Pick %, type e.g. 10, and the goal aims for 10% of the books' balance when its window opened (a weekly goal: the balance on Monday), worked out again each new week or month as the capital grows or shrinks. The editor shows the amount under the box, and the card shows "· 10% of capital" next to the target. Switching back to ₹ turns the % into today's amount. Saved on the goal as `pnlPct` and synced.
- **Withdrawals**: the **Withdraw** button on the goal card logs money taken out to your bank (book, date, amount, note), and lists or removes past ones. A goal can have a **Money withdrawn (banked)** target, so it only counts as done once the profit is actually taken out. Withdrawals lower a book's worked-out balance (a live Delta or CoinDCX wallet already shows them) and sync like trades (`withdrawals` in `journal.json`).
- **Trade inspector**: click any trade, or its **Review** button in Analysis, to open it; tap a rule, setup-checklist item or tag to switch it on or off (saved at once). Pick the setup from the playbook's names (or None) in one tap; its checklist shows even before anything on it is ticked.
- **Data** (Settings on mobile): accounts, Delta Exchange import, CoinDCX import, GitHub sync, CSV export and backups. Trades come in from Delta and CoinDCX, so logging by hand is the small **+ Log trade by hand** link on the Trading Books card; the mobile bottom bar has no + button. A book with nothing in it (no trades, starting balance or withdrawals, like an unused Dhan book) is left off the book bar; Trading Books still lists it, marked "hidden from the bar (empty)".
- **CoinDCX API import**: CoinDCX doesn't answer web pages, so GitHub reads it for you. The sync repo runs `.github/workflows/coindcx.yml` every hour, which runs `tools/coindcx-import.mjs` from this repo with the key in the sync repo's secrets (`COINDCX_API_KEY`, `COINDCX_API_SECRET`). It adds new futures transactions (INR and USDT margined) to `journal.json`; each device gets them with its next sync, in the CoinDCX book an uploaded report fills. A transaction that is also in an uploaded report counts once (same pair and kind, within 3 seconds, same net). It also reads the fills (price, side and size) a week at a time, the first week ending tomorrow: CoinDCX stops at the start of the end day, so ending at today left today's fills out. A failed run says why on the Data tab's CoinDCX card. CoinDCX has no read-only keys, so use a separate key, not bound to an IP (GitHub's servers change address).
- **Trading day (05:30)**: the day rolls over at 05:30 IST (00:00 UTC, when exchanges reset their day), not midnight, so a trade at 01:30 counts for the day before on the dashboard, calendar, equity curve, daily loss and trade limits, goals, weekly review and Analysis (journal and broker-report trades alike). The trade keeps its real date and time; only the day it counts toward moves. Change it (midnight to 07:00) on the Data tab's **Trading Day** card; it is saved on that device. An hour picked before (other than the old 2 AM default) is kept. The Signals tab and BTC market days stay on calendar days.
- **Rupees and dollars**: the ₹ INR / $ USD switch at the start of the accounts bar shows every book, tab and figure (P&L, fees, balances, limits, goals, broker reports) in one currency. Each book converts at its own rate: dollar books at Delta India's fixed ₹85, CoinDCX at its USDT price (₹99.30 by default, from its wallet ₹ ÷ $). Change a rate in Sync & Settings → Trading Books → Edit. Prices and sizes stay as traded; the trade inspector shows the book's own figure next to the converted one.

## Where data lives

- **This browser**: everything is saved in `localStorage` (`tapeAndTarget.v2` for the journal, `tapeAndTarget.signals` for alerts, `tapeAndTarget.mktHourly` for cached BTC candles). Clearing site data clears the journal.
- **Broker report uploads**: stored with the journal (`backtest` in `tapeAndTarget.v2` and in `journal.json` once something is uploaded): the report's rows only, never the cover sheet's name, email or PAN. Each uploaded file keeps the broker it belongs to; the broker's book (`rep-<broker>-<currency>`) is made from it on any device. The file is read in the browser; nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Deleted records**: a deletion leaves a marker so sync doesn't bring the record back from another device. Markers are dropped after 30 days (183 for a deleted Delta trade's order tag, which stops the Delta import re-adding it), so `journal.json` stays small. A device not synced for over 30 days could bring back something deleted since.
- **GitHub sync (optional)**: on the Data tab, add a fine-grained token with *Contents: Read and write* on your sync repo (e.g. `victus2034/trading-journal`). The journal is saved there as `journal.json`, so other devices can pick it up. The token, Delta API key and webhook never leave the browser; the CoinDCX key lives only in the sync repo's Actions secrets.
- **Delta balance across devices**: the device holding the Delta key fetches Delta's wallet figure and syncs it as `deltaBalance` in `journal.json` (the figure only, never the key; sent when the journal opens, then when it moves, at most every 10 minutes, or hourly). A device without the key, or one Delta won't answer, shows that figure instead of its own trade sum; the header tag's tooltip says "fetched … on another device".
- **Export**: the Data tab can download trades as CSV.

## Updating

Replace `index.html` and bump `<meta name="app-version">` near the top, so the version pill in the header shows which build each device is on.
Chart.js loads from jsDelivr; without it, charts are skipped and the rest still works.

## Checks

Every push runs `tests/smoke.mjs` on GitHub: it opens the page in a headless browser, with an empty journal and with made-up sample trades, on desktop and phone widths, opens every tab, and fails on any script error. It also uploads a made-up trade report (`tests/backtest-fixture.mjs`, as .xlsx and as .csv) and checks every trade and total against the report, in Analysis and on the Dashboard, plus the broker books and the per-broker results. To run it locally: `npm install`, `npx playwright install chromium`, then `npm test` (or `npm run test:twice`, which runs it twice; CI and the VS Code "Test twice" task use that). AI editors follow `AGENTS.md`: every change is tested twice before it is called done.
